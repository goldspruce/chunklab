Why AI Platforms Perform Better on Biology than Physics

Root Cause Analysis Across 10 Cross-Disciplinary Benchmark Questions
Key Insight: Across all 5 platforms, retrieval recall and quote verification are systematically higher in biology (Alberts, Lehninger, Berg Kinetics) than in physics (Feynman Vol 1 & 2, Acoustic Impedance, Skin Depth). This divergence stems from lexical specificity, derivation structure, and mathematical OCR fragility.

1. The Four Fundamental Root Causes

Biology: High-Entropy Proper Nouns

Uses unique, highly specialized vocabulary (carbonyl oxygen, selectivity filter, Briggs-Haldane, ATP synthase). Keyword search hits target pages with near-zero false-positive noise.

Physics: Low-Entropy Polysemic Words

Uses generic words (wave, boundary, reflection, energy, depth) and single-letter variables ($Z, c, \omega, \mu, \sigma$). Words collide across hundreds of unrelated pages in Feynman.

Biology: Modular Descriptive Facts

Mechanisms are self-contained in descriptive sentences (e.g., how carbonyl oxygens coordinate potassium). A single 300-word chunk holds the entire causal proof.

Physics: Multi-Page Continuous Derivations

Truth is derived across multiple pages (Wave Equation → Boundary Conditions → Density Substitution → Impedance Formula). Arbitrary text chunking cuts derivations in half.

Biology: Clean ASCII / Text OCR

Standard Latin prose scans with 99% fidelity. Quote Snapping effortlessly finds character-exact matches in textbook files on disk.

Physics: Fragile Mathematical OCR

Greek letters ($\omega \to w, \mu \to u$), fractions, square roots ($\sqrt{\cdot}$), and superscripts get corrupted by OCR, causing Gate 1 Quote Snapping to fail on real equations.

Biology: Compartmentalized Concepts

Mitochondrial ATP synthesis does not look like nuclear histone modification. Low risk of citation confusion or cross-talk.

Physics: Entangled Mathematical Form

Sound, light, and transmission lines all share identical wave equations ($\nabla^2 \psi - \frac{1}{v^2}\ddot{\psi} = 0$). Models easily confuse adiabatic sound speed with acoustic reflection (Citation Bluffing).
