## 2. Key Metric Definitions & Methodology

The scorecard evaluates each architecture through two independent evaluation standards:

### A. Method 1: Scientific Hypothesis Verification (Closed System)
- **Verdict Accuracy (%)**: Percentage of the 10 scientific inquiries where the model correctly determined whether the hypothesis was supported, refuted, or unaddressed by the literature.
- **Target Page Recall (%)**: Percentage of ground-truth textbook pages successfully located and ingested by the model's retrieval engine.

### B. Method 2: Allen Institute ScholarQA Forensic Audit (Attribution Veracity)
- **Quote Veracity / Snapping (%)**: $\frac{\text{Quotes Snapped to Real Disk Bytes}}{\text{Total Quotes Emitted}}$. Evaluates physical existence in the 12,320 textbook chunks on disk.
- **Citation Hallucination (%)**: $\frac{\text{Fabricated Quotes}}{\text{Total Quotes Emitted}}$. Quotes generated by the model that do not exist anywhere in the physical literature.
- **Citation Bluffing (%)**: $\frac{\text{Verified Real Quotes that Fail Logical Entailment}}{\text{Total Verified Quotes}}$. The percentage of real textbook quotes that the model dishonestly attached to irrelevant or false claims.

### C. Resource Efficiency
- **Context Word Consumption (Avg Words)**: The average number of context words injected into the LLM prompt per query.
- **Token Efficiency Multiplier**: How much less context each system consumes compared to the brute-force long-context caching approach ($S_{\text{cached}}$).

## 3. Executive Architectural Findings

### 1. The Raw Frontier Baseline ($S_0$): 88.9% Hallucination & Bluffing
When operating without retrieval, commercial frontier LLMs hallucinate citations in **88.9%** of cases. While they can quote common formulas (like $E=mc^2$), they invent plausible-sounding prose and fictional page numbers when asked to cite specific mechanisms, achieving only **10.0% Verdict Accuracy**.

### 2. The Sanity Engine Invariant ($S_2$ and $S_3$): 0.0% Citation Bluffing
Both $S_2$ (Relational Proposition DB) and $S_3$ (Causal Claim Graph) achieved a **0.0% Citation Bluffing Rate**. Whenever Sanity Engine provided a verified citation, the quote was 100% deductively sound and logically entailed the claim. 

### 3. Precision vs. Recall: The $S_2$ vs. $S_3$ Tradeoff
- **$S_2$ (Relational DB)** delivered the highest overall accuracy (**70.0%**) while consuming only **357 words** per query. Its structured SQL FTS5 matching excels at surfacing concrete empirical facts.
- **$S_3$ (Causal Graph)** delivered the highest quote veracity (**87.5%**) and lowest hallucination rate (**12.5%**) among targeted retrievers. Its lower accuracy score (**40.0%**) is driven by **calibrated abstention**—when an explicit causal edge is missing between chapters, it refuses to guess.

### 4. Long-Context Caching ($S_{\text{cached}}$): The $139\times$ Token Penalty
Long-context caching achieved **94.1% Quote Veracity** by dumping entire books (~50,000 words) into the prompt. However:
- It suffered a **5.9% Citation Bluffing Rate** (e.g. misattributing acoustic sound speed to boundary reflection in Question 3).
- It burned **$139\times$ more context tokens** than Sanity Engine $S_2$, resulting in high inference latency and substantial API costs without beating $S_2$'s accuracy.
